**Findings in RUNBOOK.md**

1. **RUNBOOK.md:37 (high, security):** The secret is expanded into the `sudo ... sed` command line. It lands in sudo's auth log (`COMMAND=...`) and is visible in `ps` to other users. This undercuts the "without echoing or writing to history" goal at step 7 (line 31). Fix: pass the key on stdin or through the environment instead. For example, use `printf '%s' "$NEW_KEY" | sudo -u ingest python3 -c '...'` to do the replacement, or use the `sudoedit` route for every case. Then add `unset NEW_KEY` after step 8.

2. **RUNBOOK.md:35-43 (high, missing failure path):** The service is already stopped at this point (step 5). If the step 8 grep count is not `1`, the step 8 sed fails, or the step 9 parse check fails, nothing says what to do. Rollback only triggers on step 10 or 11 failures, so the on-call engineer is left with a down service and a possibly broken `keys.yaml`. Fix: add "If the step 8 count is not 1 or the step 9 parse check fails, go to Rollback" and make Rollback apply from step 6 onward.

3. **RUNBOOK.md:44, 48 (medium, bug):** `journalctl -n 50` immediately after start has three problems. It includes lines from before the restart, so stale 401s from the old run can trigger a false rollback. It can run before the service has made its first authenticated call, which lets a bad key pass. And the bare `auth` match also hits benign lines such as "authenticated". Fix: use `journalctl -u ingest.service --since "-2min" --no-pager`, wait about 30 seconds after the start, and grep for `401|403|auth(entication)? (fail|error)`.

4. **RUNBOOK.md:58-63 (medium, missing verification):** Rollback restores the old key and starts the service but never checks `is-active`, the journal, or lag. The runbook can then end with the service down while the engineer believes it is restored. Fix: after the start, add `systemctl is-active ingest.service` and a lag check, and page at step 15 if either fails.

5. **RUNBOOK.md:29 (medium, edge case):** `cp -p` silently overwrites any existing `keys.yaml.bak`. If a previous attempt was interrupted after step 8, re-running the runbook replaces the only copy of the old key with the new key, and Rollback becomes useless. Fix: add `test ! -e /etc/ingest/config/keys.yaml.bak || { echo "stale .bak, stop"; false; }` before the copy, or use a timestamped backup name and reference it in Rollback.

6. **RUNBOOK.md:51, 71 (medium, security):** The note says to delete `keys.yaml.bak` when the old key is deleted. Neither step 12 branch does this. In the compromised case, the backup holding the compromised secret stays on disk. In the normal case, the 24-hour deletion ticket doesn't mention it. Fix: add `sudo rm /etc/ingest/config/keys.yaml.bak` to the compromised branch of step 12 and to the text of the follow-up ticket.

7. **RUNBOOK.md:56-64 (low, ops hygiene):** After a rollback, the newly created vendor key from step 4 is left active. Fix: add "delete the new key in the Vendor Console" to Rollback, after the service is confirmed healthy.
