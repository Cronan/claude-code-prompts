1. **CONFIRMED AT minor.** Steps 2 and 3 do use "the change ticket" and "ticket <id>" without naming a system or id source. That does not block anything: the on-call engineer will know their own ticket process, and nothing here prevents a step from running. It only affects wording, not the result.

2. **NOT CONFIRMED.** Step 11 says "the `ingest-prod-*` key with the earlier date". Step 4 creates the new key as `ingest-prod-YYYYMMDD`, so the old key follows the same pattern and has the earlier date. The "legacy name" and "several older keys" cases are speculation. The runbook does not assume exactly two keys, because it picks the one with the earlier date.

3. **NOT CONFIRMED.** Step 7 uses `python3 -c "import yaml..."`, and nothing in the runbook confirms PyYAML is installed, but that is not shown to be a defect. If the import failed, `OK` would not print and step 7 sends you to step 13. Because the service is stopped and the new key is not yet live, rollback is a safe no-op restore. The failure is visible and the result is a safe rollback, not a wrong result. At worst this is a minor gap.

4. **CONFIRMED AT misleading.** In step 6 the `sed` replacement is `$NEW_KEY` inside `s|...|...|`. A secret containing `|`, `&` or `\` would break or corrupt the substitution. The part of the claim about YAML-significant characters (`#`, `: `, leading `*` or `&` and so on) also holds. Step 7's `grep -qxF "api_key: $NEW_KEY"` compares the raw text, which matches, and `safe_load` succeeds, so a value that YAML parses differently from the secret passes both checks. The same corruption can also make step 7's grep fail, which sends you to rollback. Whether real vendor secrets contain such characters is unknown, so this is conditional.

5. **CONFIRMED AT misleading.** The window in step 9 is `--since "-5min"`, and step 5's stop happened before step 8's start. If step 5 to step 9 took under 5 minutes, shutdown errors could be included and give a false failure. The other half of the claim is also true: the check runs right after start, so auth errors may not have appeared yet. That could give a false pass. This is plausible but depends on timing and on what the stop logs.

6. **CONFIRMED AT misleading.** Step 13 says to repeat the step 9 checks after the restart. The `-5min` window will still include error lines from the failed new-key run, because step 9 or step 10 triggered the rollback within minutes. The re-check can therefore fail even if the restore worked, and step 15 asks you to report that result. It does not apply when step 7 triggered the rollback, since the service never started on the new key.
